# Jounit Trademarks and Brand Use

The *Jounit* name, the `JNT` ticker and the project logos are community marks
— no single person holds them, like Bitcoin.

This document describes how they may be used. It is deliberately short, because
a trademark policy that needs a lawyer to read is not a policy.

It is **not** a licence. The software is open source and licensed under
`MIT OR Apache-2.0` (see `LICENSE-MIT` and `LICENSE-APACHE`). This document
covers only the marks.

---

## The short version

- **The code is open.** Use it, modify it, run it, audit it, redistribute it,
  including commercially. The licence grants all of that.
- **The name is not free.** You may not ship a product called "Jounit", a token
  called "JNT", or something using our logos that is not this software.

This is the arrangement Bitcoin uses, and it is why the two are separated. If
both were covered by one permissive licence, "Jounit" would be free for anyone to
take — and a token name is the one thing that must never be ambiguous to a user
holding real money.

## What is covered

- The word marks **Jounit**, **JNT**, and any confusingly similar rendering
- The project logos and wordmark
- Domain names and social handles in the project's name

## What you may do without asking

- Run the software, on any network, for any purpose
- Fork it, modify it, study it, audit it
- Redistribute it, modified or not, under the same licence
- Run a public node, a testnet, or a fork of the chain
- Use it as a dependency in your own project
- Write about it, review it, report vulnerabilities in it
- Use the marks **to refer to this project factually** — saying "built on Jounit",
  linking to this repository, naming it in a compatibility list

That last one is the normal case. Talking about us is always fine; claiming to
*be* us is not.

## What you may not do without asking

- Name a product, company, chain, token or service **"Jounit"** or **"JNT"**
- Use the logos in your own product's branding
- Publish a fork under our name, or imply official affiliation
- Sell anything under our name
- Register our marks as your own, anywhere

## Forking the code

**You may fork it. Nothing in the licence stops you, and nothing here does
either.**

Forking a public blockchain is normal and expected — several chains descend from
Bitcoin, and that is not theft. What you may not do is call your fork "Jounit"
or ship it under our marks. Pick your own name and ship it as your own project.

## Using the name or marks commercially

If you want to do something with the marks that the list above does not clearly
cover — a product with JNT in the name, a service built around the chain that
wants to use our identity, a rebrand of this software — **ask first.** A short
written permission is usually all it takes.

Include a written agreement that grants the right to use the marks, with the
scope of what is permitted.

## Chain forks and the ticker

The `JNT` ticker names the asset on this chain. If you fork the chain, your fork
gets its own ticker and your own name. Two chains both showing "JNT" is exactly
the confusion that harms users, and it is the specific outcome this policy
exists to prevent.

---

## Reporting a problem

If you believe something is using the marks improperly, open a private issue or
contact the community maintainers directly. Do not open a public issue about
someone else's project.

---

## Why this document exists

Because the code is MIT-licensed, anyone can fork it, and that is the intended
behaviour — openness is the point. But a permissive code licence cannot protect a
brand, and a brand that is not protected eventually gets used by someone who is
not us, on a chain that is not this one, holding a token that looks like ours.
Users then have no way to tell the difference.

The code stays open. The name stays with the community. That is the whole
policy.
